title: "Get Started with Triton One RPC Endpoints"
description: "Create your account, understand endpoints and tokens, configure your application, and make your first Triton One RPC call in minutes."
---

Connecting your application to Triton One takes just a few minutes. This guide covers everything from creating your account to making your first API call — including the important distinction between public endpoints and secret tokens.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create an account">
    To use Triton One, you need an account with an active subscription. You have two options:

    - **Self sign-up:** Visit [customers.triton.one/onboarding](https://customers.triton.one/onboarding) and make a deposit to activate your account immediately.
    - **Contact us directly:** For custom plans or dedicated node deployments, email [support@triton.one](mailto:support@triton.one) or reach out via [Telegram](https://t.me/tritonone).

    The Triton team will help you choose between a shared service or a dedicated node based on your traffic and latency requirements.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Understand endpoints vs. tokens">
    Once your account is active, the [Customer Portal](https://customers.triton.one) gives you access to two types of credentials: **Endpoints** and **Tokens**. Understanding the difference is critical before you configure your application.

    **Endpoint URL — for frontend and dApps**

    Your endpoint URL is a public-facing address you can safely integrate into a website or dApp:

    ```
    https://your-app.mainnet.rpcpool.com
    ```

    Traffic to this URL is rate-limited by IP address and origin domain to protect against abuse. You can embed this URL in frontend code.

    **Secret token — for backend services**

    A token is a secret key for authenticating requests from backend servers, scripts, or trading bots. You append it directly to your endpoint URL:

    ```
    https://your-app.mainnet.rpcpool.com/your-secret-token
    ```

    Backend traffic authenticated with a token has higher rate limits than unauthenticated frontend traffic.

    <Warning>
      Never embed a secret token in frontend code, mobile app bundles, or any publicly accessible source. If you suspect a token has been compromised, contact [support@triton.one](mailto:support@triton.one) immediately to have it rotated.
    </Warning>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure your application">
    With your endpoint URL in hand, point your Solana SDK or HTTP client at the appropriate URL format for your use case.

    **JSON-RPC over HTTPS**

    Use your HTTPS endpoint for standard RPC calls:

    ```
    https://your-app.mainnet.rpcpool.com
    ```

    **WebSocket subscriptions**

    For real-time subscriptions, replace `https` with `wss`:

    ```
    wss://your-app.mainnet.rpcpool.com
    ```

    If you're using a backend token, append it the same way for both HTTPS and WebSocket URLs.

    <Tip>
      For high-throughput use cases or real-time data pipelines, consider using [Dragon's Mouth gRPC](/streaming/dragons-mouth-grpc) instead of WebSocket subscriptions. gRPC streams deliver intra-slot updates up to 400ms faster.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Explore the documentation">
    You're ready to build. Here are the most useful next steps depending on what you're working on:

    - **Understand platform behavior:** Read the [Core Features](/core-features) guide to learn about request routing, rate limits, and abuse prevention.
    - **Solana-specific features:** Explore [Cascade transaction delivery](/solana/cascade-transactions), the [Digital Assets API](/solana/digital-assets-api), and the [Old Faithful historical archive](/solana/old-faithful-archive).
    - **Real-time streaming:** Get started with [Dragon's Mouth gRPC](/streaming/dragons-mouth-grpc) or [Whirligig WebSockets](/streaming/whirligig-websockets).
    - **Trading tools:** Discover [Metis swap routing](/trading-apis/metis-swap), [Jito bundle simulation](/trading-apis/jito-bundles), and [Pyth Hermes price feeds](/trading-apis/pyth-hermes).
  </Step>
</Steps>
