Running a Solana validator contributes to network security and decentralization, and earns staking rewards proportional to the stake you attract. Triton's hosted validator service builds on the same infrastructure used for its top-tier RPC platform: you get the hardware, monitoring, and operational expertise, while retaining complete control over your vote account.

<Note>
  Triton owns and manages the validator identity key. You or your team control the vote account withdrawal authority. Control over the withdrawal authority means you have complete control over your vote account — including the ability to withdraw rewards and change the validator's commission.
</Note>

## What Triton manages

On every hosted validator, Triton ensures:

- Access limited to a small, trusted team
- Node identity keys stored in-memory only — never written to disk in unencrypted form
- 24/7 monitoring to ensure consistent voting and detect issues before they affect rewards

For details on how node identity key protection works, see [Identity Protection](/validators/node-identity-protection).

## Before you commit

Running a validator has ongoing costs. Make sure you understand the economics before proceeding:

1. **Vote fees** — You will pay approximately 1 SOL per day in vote transaction fees until you have attracted enough stake to break even.
2. **Break-even stake** — You need roughly 375,000 delegated SOL for transaction fee revenue to cover voting expenses and generate profit.

<Warning>
  If you only want exposure to staking rewards without the overhead of running a validator, you can stake with existing validators instead. See [validators.app](https://validators.app) for a list of active validators and stake pools.
</Warning>

## Supported validator clients

Triton validates client behavior continuously to ensure correct protocol operation. The choice of client is yours — all of the following are supported:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Agave" icon="code">
    The official reference implementation maintained by [Anza](https://anza.xyz/). Feature-complete and receives all protocol updates first. Serves as the baseline for all other clients.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Jito-Solana" icon="bolt">
    A fork of Agave maintained by [Jito Labs](https://jito.network/), integrating an MEV auction system. Validators receive transaction bundles from a private relay and earn additional rewards through MEV tips with minimal configuration overhead.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Paladin" icon="shield">
    A fork of Jito-Solana maintained by the [Paladin](https://www.paladin.one/) team. Offers stricter bundle filtering and a protected transaction pathway that mitigates frontrunning and sandwich attacks. Fully compatible with Solana consensus.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Frankendancer" icon="gauge-high">
    A hybrid client maintained by [Jump Crypto](https://jumpcrypto.com/firedancer/) combining Firedancer's networking and block ingestion stack with Agave's consensus execution. Engineered for high performance and low-latency block processing. Already in production on mainnet.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Cascade SWQoS participation

Triton operates Cascade, a prioritized transaction routing layer that gives validators access to a higher-quality stream of transactions and the opportunity to generate additional revenue from transaction fees.

Participation in Cascade SWQoS is opt-in. To receive authenticated traffic from Triton's transaction distribution layer, you configure your validator to accept it. For configuration details, see [Providing Transaction Bandwidth](/solana/cascade-transactions).
