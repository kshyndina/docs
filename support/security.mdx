With usage-based pricing, a leaked RPC token doesn't just mean unauthorized access — it means someone else's traffic shows up on your bill. This page covers how to keep your credentials secure, how Triton's systems protect your endpoints, and exactly what to do if something goes wrong.

## Tokens vs. allowed origins

Triton endpoints support two authentication methods. Using the wrong one for your context is one of the most common mistakes we see.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Secret tokens" icon="key">
    For server-side use only: Rust backends, Node.js servers, scripts, trading bots, and gRPC clients. The token is appended directly to your endpoint URL.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Allowed origins" icon="globe">
    For browser-based applications and dApps. Use the bare endpoint URL without a token, and configure a whitelist of domains in your dashboard.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Secret tokens: backend only

Your RPC token is appended directly to your endpoint URL:

```
https://<your-endpoint>.rpcpool.com/<your-secret-token>
```

When you authenticate with a token, origin validation is intentionally disabled — there is no meaningful "origin" in a server-side context. This format is designed for backend processes only.

<Warning>
  Never put your token in frontend code. If it's embedded in a browser-facing application, anyone who opens DevTools can copy it. Once it's exposed, anyone can use it to make requests that land on your bill.
</Warning>

Treat your RPC token with the same care as a database password or private key:

- Store it in environment variables, not in source code
- Never commit it to a git repository (public or private)
- Don't log it — tokens frequently end up in application logs, error traces, and observability pipelines
- Use separate tokens for production, staging, and development environments to limit blast radius if one leaks

### Allowed origins: for frontends and dApps

For browser-based applications, use the bare endpoint URL without a token, and configure your allowed origins in the [Customer Dashboard](https://customers.triton.one/):

```
https://<your-endpoint>.rpcpool.com/
```

Triton will then only serve requests from the domains you've explicitly whitelisted. Traffic from these endpoints is rate-limited by IP address and origin domain.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your Customer Dashboard">
    Log in at [customers.triton.one](https://customers.triton.one/).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Navigate to your endpoint">
    Click your subscription name, then click the endpoint name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your domain">
    Click the **+** button, enter your domain, and save changes. All subdomains are automatically allowed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  You may see `__blocked.rpcpool.com` in your allowed origins list. Do not remove it. This system entry signals that your endpoint is closed to unauthenticated requests and will only accept calls made with a valid token. Removing it and leaving the list empty makes your endpoint fully open to anyone.
</Note>

### Always use HTTPS

Always use `https` (or `wss` for WebSockets), never `http`. Even though unencrypted connections technically work, your authentication data is exposed to network snooping on the open internet over plain HTTP.

## What to do if your token leaks

Act quickly. The sooner you rotate, the smaller the window of exposure.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contact support immediately">
    Open a chat from your [Customer Dashboard](https://customers.triton.one/) or email [support@triton.one](mailto:support@triton.one). We'll rotate your token and issue a new one. The old token becomes invalid as soon as the new one is issued.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check for unusual traffic">
    Review the RPC Metrics tab in your dashboard for request volume and method breakdown. Look for unfamiliar methods, unusual traffic patterns, or a sudden jump in bandwidth since the suspected leak.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Audit your codebase">
    Search for the token string across all repos, config files, CI/CD pipelines, and environment files. GitHub's secret scanning feature can help automate this.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review your allowed origins">
    Ensure your allowed origins list is tight and doesn't include wildcard or overly broad entries.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rotate related credentials">
    If the same token was reused across environments or stored alongside other secrets, treat those as potentially compromised too.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Abuse prevention

Triton's abuse prevention systems are built into the platform — you don't need to place third-party proxies in front of our service.

<Note>
  Placing a proxy (such as Cloudflare) in front of Triton creates a man-in-the-middle that decrypts all traffic, breaks GeoDNS and Anycast routing, and disables our abuse prevention systems. When you proxy our service, you assume full responsibility for any resulting security incidents. We strongly advise against it. See our [proxying guide](/core-features) for details.
</Note>

Our multi-layered abuse prevention includes:

- **Traffic filtering at the edge** — load balancers reject malformed requests and anything that doesn't conform to valid JSON-RPC specifications before they reach backend nodes
- **Rate limiting by IP** — limits are applied per originating IP to prevent any single actor from overwhelming the service; see the [Rate Limits](/core-features) page for thresholds
- **Advanced fingerprinting** — sophisticated techniques identify and block actors attempting to spoof credentials or allowed origins
- **Early connection drops for repeat offenders** — sources generating large volumes of 403 or 429 errors will have their connections dropped silently at the network edge rather than receiving per-request error responses

If you see network-level connection failures in your logs rather than HTTP error codes, and your traffic is legitimate, check that your token is correctly configured and your allowed origins are set up properly, then back off for at least one minute before retrying.

### Rate limit headers

Shared pool responses include `X-Ratelimit-*` headers so you can monitor your current usage programmatically:

```http
X-Ratelimit-Limit: 1200
X-Ratelimit-Remaining: 843
X-Ratelimit-Reset: 1714500010
```

Check your endpoint's specific limits at:

```
https://<your-endpoint>.rpcpool.com/ratelimits
```

## Data privacy

Triton is committed to responsible data handling and full compliance with privacy regulations including GDPR.

### What Triton logs

We process three categories of customer-related data to operate our services:

- **RPC service log data** — logs generated by user interaction with RPC endpoints
- **Validator node identity keys** — for customers running validators on our infrastructure
- **Customer account and billing information** — required for account management and invoicing

### Logging levels

The logging level applied to your endpoint depends on your service type:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Shared services">
    All data collection is for abuse prevention and operational stability only. We do not identify or de-anonymize individual end-users. Standard log retention is four weeks.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Dedicated services — Max Privacy">
    No request parameters or payloads are logged. This offers the highest level of privacy but may limit our ability to detect certain types of abuse. Required for any shared infrastructure components.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Dedicated services — Enhanced Privacy (default)">
    We may log method parameters, but we cannot link the signer of a transaction to the originating IP address. This provides a balance of operational insight and user privacy.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Dedicated services — GDPR Privacy">
    We may log all parameters for technical support, legal, or compliance purposes. All data is handled in a fully GDPR-compliant manner.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### What Triton does not store

- **Private keys** — validator keys are never stored or transmitted unencrypted; they only exist in decrypted form in memory while actively in use
- **Credit card numbers** — sensitive payment details are never stored on our systems; all billing is handled by dedicated, compliant third-party payment processors

## Triton's infrastructure security

Beyond protecting your credentials, Triton operates a defense-in-depth security model across its global infrastructure:

- **Access control** — all access is governed by least-privilege IAM; MFA with hardware security tokens (Yubikeys) is required for all services
- **Encrypted network backbone** — all internal traffic travels over a private, encrypted network isolated from the public internet
- **Host firewalls** — every host is protected by a firewall with minimal exposure policy; only required ports are open
- **Automated patching** — servers receive automated security patches daily
- **Continuous auditing** — automated daily security audits on all hosts check for compliance and identify potential risks
- **24/7 monitoring** — a sophisticated alerting stack notifies our on-call team of suspicious activity or critical security events
- **Formal incident response** — documented Security Incident Response and Disaster Recovery plans based on NIST standards
