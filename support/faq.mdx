## Account & billing

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How do I get an account?">
    Sign up using the [onboarding form](https://customers.triton.one/onboarding). If you have custom infrastructure requirements or prefer to speak with the team first, email [support@triton.one](mailto:support@triton.one) or reach out on [Telegram](https://t.me/tritonone) and we'll connect you with the right setup.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do you require long-term contracts?">
    No. Standard billing is month-to-month. You're not locked into any minimum commitment unless you've negotiated a custom arrangement.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What payment methods do you accept?">
    We accept the following payment methods:

    - **USDC** — SPL, ERC20 (Ethereum), Tron, and Polygon
    - **hel.io** — USDC payments via hel.io
    - **Credit cards** — via Coinflow (available for shared service customers)
    - **Wire transfers** — in GBP and USD

    For billing questions, contact us at [support@triton.one](mailto:support@triton.one).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="When will I receive my invoice, and what period does it cover?">
    We send two types of invoices:

    - **Subscription invoices** (nodes, shared plans): sent on the first day of each month as prepayment for the upcoming month.
    - **Add-on invoices** (Cascade, Metaplex DAS, and similar metered services): sent early the following month, covering the previous month's usage.

    All invoices are due within 7 days of the issue date.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I change my plan later?">
    Yes. You can upgrade or downgrade your plan as your needs change. Contact support to discuss the right configuration for your workload.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I cancel my subscription?">
    We require one calendar month's notice for cancellation. This aligns with the notice periods from our own infrastructure vendors. To start the cancellation process, contact us through the support channel in your [Customer Dashboard](https://customers.triton.one/).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Technical

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What happens if I hit my rate limit?">
    Your application receives an `HTTP 429 Too Many Requests` response. When this happens, you **must pause all requests from that IP for 10 seconds** — retrying immediately will continue to return 429 errors and will not succeed.

    We strongly recommend implementing a backoff-and-retry mechanism in your application. The default limit on shared pools is 1200 requests per 10 seconds per IP. Computationally expensive methods such as `getProgramAccounts` have stricter limits.

    You can check your current rate limit thresholds at:

    ```
    https://<your-endpoint>.rpcpool.com/ratelimits
    ```

    Shared pool responses also include `X-Ratelimit-*` headers so you can monitor usage programmatically in real time.

    <Tip>
      If you have a dedicated node, contact support to configure custom rate limits that match your workload.
    </Tip>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I run scripts or bots on the shared service?">
    No. The shared service is designed exclusively for frontend dApp traffic. Backend scripts, bots, and automated trading tools require a dedicated node. Running high-frequency automated traffic on shared infrastructure affects stability for all users on the pool.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What should I do if my secret token is leaked?">
    Contact us immediately through the support channel in your [Customer Dashboard](https://customers.triton.one/). We will disable the compromised token and issue a new one right away. The old token becomes invalid as soon as the new one is issued.

    After rotating, audit your codebase for any remaining references to the old token across repos, config files, CI/CD pipelines, and environment files.

    <Warning>
      Never embed your secret token in frontend code. Anyone who can open browser DevTools can copy it. Use allowed origins for browser-based applications instead.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I connect to WebSockets?">
    Replace `https` with `wss` in your RPC endpoint URL:

    ```
    wss://your-endpoint.rpcpool.com
    ```

    That said, for streaming use cases on Solana, we recommend using the gRPC interface (Dragon's Mouth) instead of WebSockets. gRPC delivers lower latency, better throughput, and more reliable reconnection semantics. See the [Dragon's Mouth gRPC Subscriptions](/streaming/dragons-mouth-grpc) page for setup details.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the advantage of sending transactions through a staked validator?">
    Staked validators have access to a significantly larger, reserved connection pool for transaction forwarding. During periods of high congestion on Solana, public connection pools become highly contested and unreliable. Sending through a staked validator gives you a substantial advantage because your transactions enter the network through a prioritized lane rather than competing with the general public pool.

    See the [Cascade](/solana/cascade-transactions) page for details on how this works.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can a 'processed' transaction on Solana still be dropped?">
    Yes. The `processed` commitment level only means the transaction has been seen by the RPC node — it does not guarantee finality. A slot containing a `processed` transaction can still be dropped due to a minority fork or network congestion. A transaction is only considered final once it reaches `confirmed` or `finalized` commitment.

    Always confirm commitment level matches your application's requirements before treating a transaction as settled.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I monitor for delays on my gRPC connection?">
    Examine the `slot` field included in all gRPC messages. Compare this value against a secondary source — another subscription or a `getSlot` call — to detect lag. If you have a dedicated node, the Grafana dashboard includes a "Tracking Tip" folder where you can see your node's slot latency relative to the rest of the network.

    If your stream appears to be running significantly behind, check whether your receiving server is applying back pressure to the RPC node due to bandwidth constraints.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I use gRPC from a web browser?">
    No. gRPC is not supported in web browsers due to HTTP/2 and protobuf limitations. It is designed for backend applications. For browser-based use cases, use [Whirligig WebSockets](/streaming/whirligig-websockets) instead.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Solana-specific

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How can I use Geyser plugins?">
    Geyser plugins are available on dedicated Solana nodes. They provide powerful real-time streams of on-chain data with flexible filtering. Contact our support team to discuss your specific requirements and we'll configure a node for you.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is Cascade?">
    Cascade is Triton's stake-weighted quality of service (SWQoS) transaction network — a bandwidth marketplace where trusted validators sell SWQoS bandwidth to consumers. When you send transactions through Cascade, they are routed through validators with staked connections, giving them priority delivery during network congestion.

    See the [Cascade](/solana/cascade-transactions) page for full details.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is Project Yellowstone?">
    Project Yellowstone is Triton's suite of high-performance, open-source tools for enhanced Solana infrastructure. It addresses real-time data streaming, historical ledger access, ultra-low latency queries, and custom indexing. Key components include:

    | Tool | What it does |
    | --- | --- |
    | **Dragon's Mouth** | Core gRPC streaming engine — delivers real-time on-chain data with ultra-low latency using Protobuf |
    | **Whirligig** | WebSocket streaming layer — designed for frontend or browser-based apps |
    | **Fumarole** | Persistent streaming with automatic redundancy and 23-hour caching — no data loss if the connection drops |
    | **Vixen** | Parser that delivers decoded JSON instead of raw binary, simplifying client integration |
    | **Steamboat** | Custom indexing engine that accelerates heavy `getProgramAccounts` queries by up to 99% |
    | **Old Faithful** | Full-ledger archival service for Solana's complete historical transaction data |
    | **Shield** | Anti-MEV protection layer with configurable allow- and block-lists |
    | **Jet** | Transaction submission software that adds stake weight to route transactions through a priority lane |

    See the [Project Yellowstone](/streaming/overview) documentation for full details on each component.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My transactions aren't landing. What should I do?">
    Triton ensures reliable delivery to the network, but for optimal transaction finalization, you should configure appropriate compute unit budgets and priority fees. Review the [sending transactions guide](/solana/cascade-transactions) for current best practices on CU budgets and priority fees.

    If you need priority routing during congestion, consider using [Cascade](/solana/cascade-transactions).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I use Jito sendBundle with Triton RPCs?">
    This is not available on shared infrastructure. On dedicated nodes, we can install the Jito software for you. You'll be responsible for whitelisting with the block engine and contacting [Jito](https://discord.gg/jito) to obtain access and complete their onboarding.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## RPC error codes

Understanding what error codes mean helps you resolve issues quickly without opening a support ticket. Follow this process when troubleshooting:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Verify your setup">
    Confirm you're using the correct endpoint URL, authorization token, and request format. Check that your request parameters match the [Solana JSON-RPC spec](https://solana.com/docs/rpc).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Isolate the error">
    Reproduce the same request against the Solana public RPC (`https://api.mainnet-beta.solana.com`). If the error occurs there too, it's likely a Solana-level issue rather than a Triton configuration problem.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match the error code">
    Use the tables below to identify what the code means and how to respond.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contact support if needed">
    If you can't resolve the issue, open a chat from your [Customer Dashboard](https://customers.triton.one/) and include: your endpoint URL, authentication method, service type (HTTP/WebSocket/gRPC), the error code, when it occurred (with timezone), and your expected result.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Common Solana JSON-RPC codes

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Standard JSON-RPC error codes">
    | Error code | Suggested action |
    | --- | --- |
    | `-32001` | Ensure your transaction uses the latest Solana transaction version. |
    | `-32002` | Ensure all required signatures are present and valid. |
    | `-32003` | Verify your keypair, rebuild the transaction with a current blockhash, sign it immediately before submission, and simulate first. |
    | `-32601` | Method not found — the method is invalid or doesn't exist. Verify it's enabled in your RPC configuration. |
    | `-32602` | Invalid method parameters. Double-check your request format against the Solana JSON-RPC spec. |
    | `-32603` | Internal JSON-RPC error. Retry after a short delay. |
    | `-32604` | The `id` field is missing from the request. Check your configuration. |
    | `-32700` | JSON parse error. Check your JSON structure. |

    For the full list of Solana error codes, see the [Anza kit repository](https://github.com/anza-xyz/kit/blob/17e3eceb7961fcc2963dc4f975d297dc22d03a59/packages/errors/src/codes.ts#L39).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### HTTP and gRPC error codes

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="HTTP 4xx client errors">
    | Code | Meaning | What to do |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `400` | Malformed request or invalid routing | Validate your request syntax and parameters |
    | `402` | Payment required | Check your account billing status |
    | `403` | Access denied | Verify your token and check that your node pool is correctly configured |
    | `404` | Resource not found | Verify the resource exists; common with Pythnet price requests |
    | `405` | Method not allowed | Review your RPC documentation for supported methods |
    | `408` | Request timeout | Check your network connection; reduce request size; retry after a short delay |
    | `410` | Resource gone | Check the changelog in your customer portal; switch to supported alternatives |
    | `413` | Payload too large | Optimize your transaction, remove unnecessary instructions, or split into multiple transactions |
    | `429` | Rate limit exceeded | Pause requests for 10 seconds; implement backoff-and-retry; upgrade to a private pool if needed |
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="HTTP 5xx server errors">
    | Code | Meaning | What to do |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `500` | Internal server error | Retry after a short delay; contact support if it persists |
    | `502` | Bad gateway | Retry the request; priority routing should recover; contact support if it persists |
    | `503` | Service unavailable | Retry after a delay; contact support if it persists |
    | `504` | Gateway timeout | Retry after a delay; resubmit with a higher priority fee; reconnect your WebSocket |
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="gRPC status codes">
    | Status | Meaning | What to do |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` | Rate limit or memory limit hit | Wait 10–15 seconds and retry; check usage at [customers.triton.one](https://customers.triton.one/); request a higher limit |
    | `UNAVAILABLE` | Server down or network issue | Retry with exponential backoff; check server health |
    | `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` | Deadline expired before response | Increase your timeout, optimize the request, or check network latency |
    | `UNAUTHENTICATED` | Invalid credentials | Verify your API key and IP whitelist; ensure credentials match your channel settings |
    | `NOT_FOUND` | Resource doesn't exist or isn't synced | Verify the resource ID; check your endpoint configuration |
    | `INTERNAL` | Internal server error | Retry after a short delay; contact support with details |
    | `CANCELLED` | Call was cancelled | Check your client code for premature cancellation |
    | `UNKNOWN` | Unhandled error | Retry the request; check your customer portal logs; report to support with details |
    | `UNIMPLEMENTED` | Method not found or unsupported | Check your protobuf definitions against Triton's; verify method support |
    | `Stream removed` | Connection closed, not just stream | Establish a new connection — reconnecting the stream alone will not work |
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
